In August 1883 the volcanic island of Krakatoa, in the Sunda Strait between Java and Sumatra, exploded. People on Rodrigues, 4,800 km away, heard it and thought it was cannon fire; the ash rose about 80 km. More than 36,000 people died, most of them in the tsunamis, 165 villages and towns were destroyed — and two thirds of the island were gone.
